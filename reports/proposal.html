Food Recipe Recommendation System

Phase 1 Project Proposal · MDS301 Machine Learning in Data Sciences · 27 October 2026
Team: Bui Minh Hoang, Tran Tuan Hung, Ho Minh Hieu (roll numbers: TBD) · Repository: github.com/minhhoangb/food-recipe-recommendation-system

1. Problem statement and users

Home cooks often have a handful of ingredients in the fridge and no idea what to make with them, so food is wasted or a meal is bought instead. Searching recipe sites by dish name does not help when the starting point is "I have chicken thighs, rice and soy sauce". Our system takes the ingredients a user has and returns the recipes that best use them, each with the ingredients the user already has (matched), those still needed (missing), the directions and a link to the source.

Who benefits: people cooking at home who want to use up what they have (students, families, anyone reducing food waste), and recipe or grocery apps that could embed the API. The scope is English recipes and ingredient-list queries; dietary, allergy or nutrition guarantees are out of scope and the interface must say so (no allergy filtering is claimed).

2. ML formulation

Task type: content-based recommendation framed as ranked retrieval (NLP). Given a set of ingredient names q, rank the 50,000 candidate recipes so the most usable ones come first. There are no users, ratings or clicks in the data, so collaborative filtering and matrix factorisation do not apply; there is no fixed label per recipe, so plain classification or regression do not fit either. Each recipe is an item described by text (title, ingredient list), which makes this a content-based, text-similarity problem.

  • Classical models (scikit-learn): TF-IDF + cosine similarity (E01; E02 = TF-IDF tuned with 5-fold group cross-validation), BM25 (E03, rank-bm25) and LSA, i.e. TruncatedSVD over TF-IDF (dimensionality reduction, unsupervised).
  • Deep learning: the transformer bi-encoder sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2, first pretrained (E04: ingredients only, E05: title + ingredients), then fine-tuned on our training pairs with MultipleNegativesRankingLoss (E07-E09, including a regularisation ablation).
  • Labels: (1) synthetic queries: 2-6 ingredients sampled from a recipe, at least one ingredient hidden and at least one non-common ingredient kept; the origin recipe is the known positive, so Recall@k / MRR@10 measure an origin-recipe retrieval proxy; (2) manual graded labels (0/1/2) on 40 natural English queries (20 development, 20 blind final), pooled across models, for nDCG@10.
  • Leakage control: recipes sharing an ingredient set or a title form a group; groups never cross the train / validation / test split. Vectorisers, IDF and fine-tuning see train only; test is read once, after the configuration is frozen.

3. Dataset

RecipeNLG (Bien et al., INLG 2020), recipenlg.cs.put.poznan.pl (also mirrored on Kaggle). One CSV of 2.29 GB with 2,231,142 recipes and 7 columns: index, title, ingredients (raw lines), directions, link, source (Gathered or Recipes1M) and NER (ingredient names extracted by the authors). The raw file stays unmodified in data/raw/ (sha256 f376ee0b…); it is not committed and is fetched or rebuilt with the documented scripts.

Subset (within the course limit of 2,000-500,000 samples). A seeded random draw (seed 42) over all raw ids, because the file is sorted by source and its first rows would be biased. Of 50,360 recipes examined, 180 were dropped as exact duplicates and 180 for having fewer than 2 or more than 40 normalised ingredients, giving 50,000 recipes (main; the first 5,000 form a pilot profile).

Features. 7 raw columns; models use the normalised ingredient list (vocabulary of 11,901 names fitted on train), the title for E05, and two engineered features (ingredient count, mean ingredient rarity fitted on train) plus query-time matched/missing counts. How the course's "8-200 features" rule applies to text input: TBD with the instructor.

Known data quality issues (raw scan and a manual review of 100 recipes, reports/data_audit.md):

  • Noisy ingredient names: 36 of 100 reviewed recipes have at least one wrong or truncated normalised ingredient (e.g. NER keeps "virgin" and loses "coconut oil"; brand fragments like "dew").
  • Missing entries: NER has fewer entries than the raw lines in 20.2% of recipes; 573 raw recipes have an empty NER list (raw-line fallback used); 1 null title.
  • Duplicates and near-duplicates: 1,245 exact duplicates in the raw file (0.06%); near-duplicates are grouped, not removed, so they cannot leak across splits.
  • Skew: 60% of distinct names occur in one recipe only, while salt appears in 43.5% of recipes. Links lack a scheme (the pipeline adds https://).

6. Risks

  • Proxy labels reward finding one recipe and may underrate good alternatives → manual graded labels (criterion 3).
  • Fine-tuning may not beat lexical baselines (on validation BM25 already leads) → reported as is; the deployed model is chosen on validation, not on expectations.
  • Free Colab GPU limits → checkpoint/resume and a CPU path with fewer steps.
  • Dependency conflicts between training and serving packages → pinned, separately tested requirements.
