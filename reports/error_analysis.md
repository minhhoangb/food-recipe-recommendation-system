# Error analysis: main validation, CPU one-epoch FT

Measured on 2026-10-29 using 500 validation queries and the existing 50,000-recipe
corpus. FT uses `models/minilm_finetuned-main-cpu1ep` for query encoding and
`models/minilm_finetuned-E04-main-1f1f4ce5` for corpus embeddings.
MLflow evidence: `d069e2ae76454826ae848f148eade5f7`. No test retrieval metrics were computed.

## Findings

- Fine-tuning raises MRR@10 from 0.4342 to 0.6209,
  with 196 improved, 30 regressed
  and 274 tied queries. BM25 remains higher at
  0.7583; TF-IDF reaches 0.6567.
- Short queries remain ambiguous: FT MRR@10 is 0.389 for two ingredients
  (122 queries), compared with 0.844 for six ingredients (81 queries).
  For `black bean; cumin` (`q:1075230`), all systems miss the origin recipe
  *Black And White Bean Chili*. Retrieved bean soups and dips illustrate why
  recovering one origin is a stricter and different goal than finding useful dishes.
- Long origin recipes remain difficult: FT reaches 0.453 for recipes with more
  than 10 ingredients (131 queries), versus 0.738 for recipes with at most six
  ingredients (144 queries). Partial ingredient queries leave many unspecified
  ingredients; this is a plausible source of ambiguity, not a demonstrated cause.
- Lexical specificity still helps BM25: for 58 queries whose rarest ingredient
  has train frequency 1-5, BM25 MRR@10 is 0.966 and FT is 0.787. In
  `q:1006155` (`anise oil; cinnamon; vinegar; flour`), BM25 ranks the origin first
  while FT misses it in the top 10. Across validation, there are 21 queries where
  BM25 ranks the origin first and FT misses it; FT finds an origin BM25 misses
  on seven other queries. BM25 uses candidate-corpus IDF, including ingredients
  unseen in train, so its unseen-ingredient bucket is not a train-vocabulary test.
- FT misses the origin in 101 queries. On 77 queries,
  its first result is a different recipe containing every normalized query
  ingredient. Ingredient containment does not establish relevance: intent,
  extra ingredients and usability require the human annotation guide.
- All seven systems miss the origin on 32 queries. The 28 cases below are
  deterministic examples from contrasting categories, not a random prevalence estimate.

## Interpretation and next evidence

The synthetic qrels assign relevance to the origin recipe; they do not certify
other results as irrelevant. Use the blind dev assignments in
[manual_eval/README.md](manual_eval/README.md) before claiming an improvement in
human relevance. Human labels, agreement and pooled nDCG@10 are still pending.
The single checkpoint and seed do not measure variation between training runs.

The query-length table includes all 500 queries after normalization. One query,
`q:706803`, collapses from two raw strings (`basket; pie shell`) to one normalized
ingredient; it now has its own length-1 row. Its FT reciprocal rank is 0.5.
